Sound tables: build a syllable

Use these as a reference while listening. The German labels Anlaut, Zwischenlaut, and Auslaut describe different positions in a syllable. A final is the whole part after the initial, so it can contain several sounds.

The parts of a syllable
PartWhat it doesExample
Initial · AnlautThe opening consonant, if present.d in duàn
Medial · ZwischenlautA short glide before the main vowel, in the Mandarin breakdown.u in duàn
Nucleus · VokalkernThe main vowel sound.a in duàn
Coda · AuslautThe closing sound, if present.n in duàn
Final · ReimEverything after the initial, excluding tone.uan in duàn
Tone · TonThe syllable’s pitch pattern.The falling fourth tone in duàn

For example, 段 duàn breaks down as d + u + a + n + tone 4. Not every syllable needs every part. Cantonese is described below using initials, vowels, and codas, without imposing Mandarin’s medial categories on it.

In Cantonese jan4 (人, “person”), j is the initial, a is the vowel, n is the coda, and 4 is the tone. The whole final is an.

Mandarin Pinyin: initials, medials, finals, and spelling rules

21 consonant initials · Anlaute

The symbols in square brackets are IPA sound symbols. Pairs such as b/p, d/t, and g/k mainly contrast a small release of air with a stronger puff, rather than following the English voiced/unvoiced distinction.

What about y and w? They help spell syllables without a consonant initial: yi, ya, wu, wa, yu. They are not extra members of the 21-initial list. A vowel can also start a syllable directly, as in ài (爱, “love”).

Three medials · Zwischenlaute

When these occur before another vowel, move quickly through them into the main vowel. The same written letters can also serve as the main vowel in other syllables.

Spelling rules that hide the parts

Common Pinyin spelling changes
RuleWhat you see
i without a consonant initiali → yi, ia → ya, ie → ye, iao → yao, iou → you, ian → yan, in → yin, iang → yang, ing → ying, iong → yong.
u without a consonant initialu → wu, ua → wa, uo → wo, uai → wai, uei → wei, uan → wan, uen → wen, uang → wang, ueng → weng.
ü without a consonant initialü → yu, üe → yue, üan → yuan, ün → yun. The sound stays rounded even though the dots disappear.
Shortened finals after an initialiou → iu (liù), uei → ui (shuǐ), uen → un (chūn).
ü after j, q, xThe dots are omitted: ju, qu, xu contain the ü sound. Keep the dots after n/l: nǚ differs from nǔ, and lǜ from lù. In jun/qun/xun, written un represents ün.
Two special uses of iIn zi, ci, si and zhi, chi, shi, ri, i does not sound like the vowel in mǐ. Keep the tongue near the preceding consonant’s position; listen to each group.
Tone-mark positionMark a first, otherwise e; in ou, mark o. In iu/ui, mark the last vowel: liù, shuǐ. A marked i loses its dot.
Apostrophe between syllablesBefore a following a-, o-, or e-initial syllable, an apostrophe can clarify the boundary: Xī’ān has two syllables; xiān has one.
er and an added -rér is a syllable of its own. In erhua, -r colours the preceding final, as in huār (花儿); do not automatically add another syllable.

Rare extras: the conventional 36-final teaching list does not cover every interjection. You may also encounter ê and syllabic m, n, ng. Learn those in context rather than treating them as freely interchangeable finals.

A syllable can also have no initial, as in aa3 (亞). Unlike the Pinyin spelling helpers, Jyutping j and w are initials. gw and kw each count as one rounded consonant initial, not g/k plus a separate Mandarin-style medial.

Nine vowel spellings · Vokalkerne

Jyutping does not use Pinyin’s three-medial table. Start with these vowel spellings, then check how they combine with endings. Vowel quality can shift with the ending; the same letter is not a promise of one identical sound in every final.
